## Game in Analysis - Resident Evil Requiem 


For this analysis, I chose **Resident Evil Requiem**, a game from a franchise I have followed for years. Even though I watched the full playthrough instead of playing it myself, I was fully engaged. Resident Evil has many parts, and what keeps it interesting is how it keeps changing while still staying true to what made it special in the first place.

In this analysis, I will focus more on the gameplay decisions rather than just the story. (As  that would be too long and hard to understand since it has long history xd!)

## What Decisions Make the Game Interesting?

### 1. Bringing Back the Past

One strong decision the designers made was bringing back older locations and characters. Places like Raccoon City and the orphanage connect the new game to the older ones.

This is not just about nostalgia. It helps players feel connected because they remember these places and what happened there. It builds emotional attachment without needing long explanations. Returning enemy types also help because players already understand how dangerous they are.

It shows that the designers respect the history of their own game.

---

### 2. Leon’s Combat Design

Leon is one of my favorite fictional characters. What makes him stand out is not just his personality but how he plays.

His combat style is very versatile:

* Guns
* Kicks
* Axe attacks
* Smooth finishing moves

He feels strong and skilled, which makes sense because he has grown from a rookie police officer into an experienced agent.

The transition between gameplay and cutscenes is seamless. It feels natural and cinematic.

**Another small but important detail is his one-liners during fights** [LEON'S ONE-LINERS](https://youtu.be/5wI9br1wj0o?si=opAYWGWtaQpK-RTX). These lines add personality and make intense situations feel stylish instead of just dark and heavy.

---

### 3. Switching Between Leon and Grace

The game splits time between:

* Leon (third-person)
* Grace (first-person)

This changes the feeling of the gameplay.

Third-person with Leon:

* Feels powerful
* Allows more movement and combat freedom
* Makes fights look stylish

First-person with Grace:

* Feels more vulnerable
* Creates tension
* Makes horror more personal

This was a bold design choice. It creates variety, but it also affects pacing (which I’ll talk about later).

---

### 4. Enemy Variety

Another strong decision was increasing the variety of enemies.

We see:

* Standard zombies
* Mutating enemies
* Lickers
* Spiders
* A larger stalker-type enemy (Emily’s sister)

This keeps the gameplay from becoming repetitive. Different enemies require different strategies (like for the lickers, Grace had to use Acid Bottle rather than shooting them for a quick kill). Some are fast, some are aggressive, and some create suspense just by existing in the area.

The “big sister” zombie especially adds tension because she feels unpredictable and dangerous.

---

### 5. Attention to Detail

The game looks extremely polished.

Small details stand out:

* Smooth animation when switching between torch and handgun
* Blood staying on Leon’s axe after killing enemies
* Detailed skin and environmental effects

These details make the world feel real and believable. They show that the designers cared about immersion.

---

## Where Have They Struggled? (Mechanically)

I wouldn’t say the game failed, but there are areas where it felt weaker.

### 1. Less Horror Compared to Other Entries

Compared to games like:

* Resident Evil 4
* Resident Evil Village

Requiem felt less terrifying overall.

Leon is very strong, so his sections feel more action-focused than horror-focused. While that makes combat exciting, it reduces the feeling of fear.

Grace’s sections try to bring back that horror feeling, but after watching intense and stylish Leon fights, switching to slower and more cautious gameplay sometimes felt like a drag.

The contrast is interesting, but it affects pacing.

---

### 2. Two Endings

The game has two endings, but only one is canon.

One ending leads to a major character’s death. The other gives closure and resolves many story points.

I understand why they added a choice, but since only one ending is officially part of the story, the choice feels less meaningful. It might have been stronger to commit to one clear ending.

---

## What Would I Borrow for My Own Projects?

### 1. Consistency with Improvement

If I ever design a game with multiple parts, I would:

* Keep important characters consistent
* Bring back familiar elements
* Improve mechanics instead of completely changing them

Players connect with what they recognize.

---

### 2. Mechanics That Match the Character

Leon’s fighting style reflects who he has become. That’s powerful.

I would design characters so that:

* Their abilities match their history
* Their combat style reflects their personality

Gameplay should show growth, not just tell it.

---

### 3. Perspective as a Tool

Switching between first-person and third-person changes how the player feels.

I would use camera perspective carefully to:

* Increase tension
* Show power
* Control pacing

But I would make sure the transitions feel balanced.

---

## Final Thoughts

Resident Evil Requiem shows how a long-running franchise can grow while staying connected to its roots.

Its strengths include:

* Strong character design
* Combat variety
* Enemy diversity
* High visual detail
* Respect for past games

After binge-watching the walkthrough, I was reminded why I’ve followed this franchise for years. It respects its characters and its players.
Good job, Capcom.
